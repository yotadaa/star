- Central thesis: X's replacement program is an institutional response to an incentive it publicly described as misaligned. The old program rewarded Premium-user reach; the new one still pays for qualified Premium impressions but with an originality classifier and explicit exclusions for copied, minimally changed, aggregated, and cross-platform reposted work.
- Boundary: the opened record shows repost and clickbait gaming and X's own diagnosis, but it cannot isolate revenue sharing as the sole cause of repost culture or establish how accurately the not-yet-rolled-out review system will classify original work.
- Counter-thesis: curation, quotation, remix, and fast commentary can add real value; an originality gate can protect authors while also misclassifying legitimate transformation or centralizing opaque editorial power.
- CTA: inspect X's current originality rules and compare them with the content a creator actually publishes before applying.

## Story spine

2. Separate old-program mechanics from X's later diagnosis.
3. Trace the March–April attempts to tune incentives and the August reset.
4. Show what counts and does not count as original.
