# Hook scorecard

Scores use a 1–5 scale. A useful opening needs a specific fact, clear stakes, an honest boundary, and a reason to keep reading.

| Candidate | Specificity | Stakes | Honesty | Reader payoff | Total | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| “A focused `/compact` removed 8,993 tokens from the active transcript. Claude then changed three files without rereading one of them, and a hidden checker found that all ten agreed decisions survived.” | 5 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 19 | Selected; the one-run boundary follows immediately. |
| “A new session passed the visible tests and still failed eight of ten hidden decisions.” | 5 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 18 | Strong secondary lead, but it can mislead before the missing-requirements explanation. |
| “Claude Code can remember a task after forgetting most of the conversation.” | 3 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 14 | Too anthropomorphic and less precise than the measured boundary. |
| “The real danger in `/compact` is not summary loss; it is invisible state.” | 3 | 4 | 2 | 4 | 13 | Overstates a verdict before the evidence is introduced. |

Selected opening boundary: identify the CLI build, non-public route, one-run proxy, and narrow research question in the next paragraph. Do not turn a 10/10 fixture result into a product-wide quality claim.
