# Hook scorecard

Four materially different hooks, drafted before the article and scored 0–2 on
five axes: factual anchoring, concrete specificity, real tension, reader
relevance, promise accuracy. Threshold for use is 8/10.

## A — Contradiction

> For six days, the model handling the largest share of OpenRouter's coding
> traffic had no maker's name attached to it.

| Axis | Score | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Factual anchoring | 2 | Route listed 2026-08-20 (S01), reveal 2026-08-26 (S05); share from C16 |
| Concrete specificity | 1 | "Largest share" is accurate but the number is the interesting part and it is missing |
| Real tension | 2 | The gap between scale and anonymity is the story's own tension |
| Reader relevance | 1 | Tells a reader what happened, not why it matters to them |
| Promise accuracy | 2 | The article does explain the anonymity and its end |
| **Total** | **8** | Usable, but weaker than D on relevance |

## B — Concrete moment

> Z.ai's release post closes a week-old question in a single sentence: "Before
> release, Z.ai tested it anonymously as `ox-alpha` on OpenCode and OpenRouter."

| Axis | Score | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Factual anchoring | 2 | Verbatim first-party quotation, C01 |
| Concrete specificity | 2 | Names the source, the sentence, and both platforms |
| Real tension | 1 | Resolves the tension in the first line and leaves nothing pulling forward |
| Reader relevance | 1 | Matters to someone already following the question |
| Promise accuracy | 2 | Accurate |
| **Total** | **8** | Strong evidence, weak forward motion |

| Axis | Score | Note |
|---|---|---|

## D — Answer first (**chosen**)

> Ox Alpha was GLM-5.3-Flash. Z.ai says so in its own release post. What that
> sentence does not settle is whether the model people spent a week testing is
> the model that shipped.

| Axis | Score | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Factual anchoring | 2 | C01 verified first-party; C04 verified as unstated across every source examined |
| Concrete specificity | 1 | Names both models and the source but carries no figure |
| Real tension | 2 | The unresolved half is genuine and is what the article actually delivers |
| Reader relevance | 2 | A reader choosing a model wants the answer and its limit in that order |
| Promise accuracy | 2 | The article answers the identity question and holds the checkpoint question open |
| **Total** | **9** | Chosen |

## Why D

The identity is verified, so withholding it would manufacture suspense the
evidence does not support. D states the answer in the first six words and spends
its tension on the one question that is genuinely open — which is also the
question the previous article left open on 2026-08-24 (C05). Approaches A and C
both score 8 but promise a different article: A a chronology, C a data-governance
piece. B resolves everything in one line and leaves no reason to read on.

## Title

Chosen: **Ox Alpha Was GLM-5.3-Flash: What Z.ai Confirmed and What It Didn't**
(66 characters, no author suffix — the article route appends `· Mukhtada`.)

The title states the verified identity plainly and bounds the rest, as required
when identity is verified. It makes no performance claim, because performance is
split between vendor claims and independent measurement and cannot be summarised
in a title without distortion.
