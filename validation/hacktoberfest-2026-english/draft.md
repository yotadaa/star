# Hacktoberfest 2026 Changed Completely: No PR Counter, New AI-Focused Format

The four-pull-request rule is gone from Hacktoberfest 2026. From September preparation through October events, the program points contributors toward open-source AI, open-weight models, and shared building rather than a number on a profile.

## The 2026 change in 60 seconds

The official Hacktoberfest site says the 2026 program will run throughout October with more than 300 online and in-person events worldwide. Major League Hacking, DEV, and DigitalOcean describe the theme as “AI belongs to everyone.” Suggested work includes writing a `skills.md` file, building an open-source agent, fine-tuning an open-weight model, or choosing another open-source AI project.

That is a different participation model. The official FAQ says participation will not center on counting individual contributions. A contributor can still make pull requests during October, but the official 2026 program does not present four accepted PRs as the milestone.

## Preptember comes first: September 1–30

Preptember is the preparation period from September 1 through September 30. The FAQ frames it around organizers planning their Fests before the October program begins. The phrase does not describe an extra PR window or an early reward period.

October then carries the global online campaign and the local event schedule. MLH’s Global Hack Week Hacktoberfest is listed for October 9–15, 2026. It is free to join online, uses Discord for community updates, and is open to beginners as well as experienced developers.

## Is four pull requests still required?

No—not for the 2026 Hacktoberfest format. The official FAQ and MLH announcement both describe a move away from the PR counter. The old number remains easy to encounter because community repositories still carry it. The [Devover Hacktoberfest repository](https://github.com/devoverid/hacktoberfest), for example, still describes four quality pull requests between October 1 and October 31. That repository documents its own community event and is not the 2026 global rulebook.

| Earlier guidance | Hacktoberfest 2026 |
|---|---|
| Four PRs were used as the familiar participation target | The official program does not center participation on a PR count |
| A T-shirt was tied to contribution milestones in historical campaigns | Online participants are not eligible for T-shirts; in-person supply is not guaranteed per person |
| Individual repository contributions were the visible unit | Events, learning, experimentation, and open-source AI projects are the visible unit |
| Local guidance often repeated the same number | The official FAQ and event gallery are the current references |

## Why did the PR counter disappear?

The organizers connect the change to two pressures. First, low-effort pull requests created noise and maintainer burnout. Second, AI tools make it easier to produce a contribution that satisfies a checklist without adding useful work. The 2026 design puts more weight on a learning environment, collaboration, and a project that can be discussed with other people.

The change has a tradeoff. A counter was easy to understand and gave beginners a concrete finish line. An event-and-project model can produce better conversations, but it asks contributors to choose a worthwhile scope and show what was learned. A contributor who needs a simple number will find the new format less mechanically clear.

## Online and in-person participation are different

The official FAQ defines a Fest as an official in-person event lasting up to 12 hours. Two formats are listed:

- A Hack Day is a structured mini-hackathon with project submissions, judging, prize categories, DEV Badges, and possible food-and-beverage reimbursement for approved organizers.
- A Meet Up is a flexible gathering for talks, workshops, panels, or social discussion. It receives official swag but does not receive the Hack Day prize or reimbursement package.

More than 300 Fests are planned across the MLH network, while the online campaign runs through DEV during October. The distinction matters for both access and rewards: the format is not one event with identical benefits in every location.

## Does every participant get a T-shirt?

No. The FAQ says online participants are not eligible for T-shirts. In-person Fests receive shipments containing stickers, T-shirts, and other materials, but local organizers distribute them according to availability and their discretion. A shirt is not guaranteed for every person, and an attendee who misses one generally should not expect a later mailing.

Online participants may earn an official Hacktoberfest Swag Envelope by completing online milestones. The organizers have not published those milestone details yet. Any article or post claiming the exact 2026 online threshold is filling a gap that the FAQ leaves open.

## A practical route from Indonesia

Indonesian contributors can use the official event gallery and MLH registration pages as the source of truth for local dates. Until an Indonesia event appears in that official listing, a tracker should show “not listed” rather than inventing a city, host, venue, or registration link.

The online route is clearer. The free Global Hack Week Hacktoberfest runs October 9–15, and the wider Hacktoberfest campaign runs throughout October. A contributor can prepare a small open-source AI project in September, then join the online event, its Discord, or an official local Fest when a listing becomes available.

Three projects fit the published examples without turning the event into a shallow checklist:

1. A `skills.md` file can explain how an agent should use a repository or a repeatable workflow.
2. An open-source agent can connect a narrow task to documented tools, tests, and failure cases.
3. An open-weight model experiment can record the model, data, prompt or training setup, evaluation method, and limits.

Each project benefits from a README, a license, a short demo, and an issue that names the next improvement. The point is a useful artifact that another participant can inspect, not an artificially padded contribution count.

## FAQ for older articles

### Must a contributor open four pull requests?

No. Four PRs describe older guidance, not the center of the official 2026 format.

### Can contributors still make pull requests?

Yes. Open-source contribution remains available year-round, but the 2026 Hacktoberfest program does not use the old counter as its defining milestone.

### Can an online participant claim a T-shirt?

No. The FAQ excludes online participants from T-shirts. Online milestones may qualify a participant for a Swag Envelope, but the criteria are forthcoming.

### Are in-person shirts guaranteed?

No. Supplies are sent to Fests and distributed locally according to availability.

### Is Preptember a competition?

No. It is the September 1–30 preparation period described for organizers.

### Where should a contributor find the current rules?

The [official FAQ](https://hacktoberfest.com/questions/) and [Hacktoberfest home page](https://hacktoberfest.com/) are stronger references than a recycled four-PR tutorial.

## A new kind of finish line

Hacktoberfest 2026 replaces a familiar counter with a calendar, a project, and a community setting. That makes the event less convenient for anyone looking for a single number, while giving contributors more room to learn open-source AI in public. The most reliable plan from Indonesia is simple: prepare in Preptember, verify the official event listing, join the free online week if no local event is available, and treat every reward claim as provisional until the FAQ publishes its missing details.
