# Assignment

Investigate the claim that OpenAI restored a five-hour limit for ChatGPT Plus
subscribers using ChatGPT Work and Codex. The article must establish who made
the announcement, when the policy was announced and implemented, what the
five-hour label measures, why OpenAI says it restored the window, and what the
change costs users whose work arrives in bursts.

## Reader question

Did OpenAI bring back a five-hour limit for Plus, and does that mean a Plus
subscriber receives five literal hours of Codex or ChatGPT Work?

## Deliverable boundary

- Form: evidence-led product-policy investigation with a practical explainer.
- Audience: Plus subscribers who use Codex or ChatGPT Work and need to plan
  sessions without assuming that elapsed time equals allowance consumption.
- Language and voice: `en-US`, warm and restrained third-person prose.
- Research cutoff: 30 August 2026, 23:59 Asia/Jakarta (UTC+7).
- Author: Mukhtada Billah NST, `https://me.mukhtada.my.id/#person`.
- Editorial section: `AI Investigation`.
- Primary `sourceHref`: Tibo Sottiaux's original announcement at
  `https://x.com/thsottiaux/status/2092058556707344708`.
- Status: draft only. This package does not authorize publication, live data
  changes, shared-manifest edits, a commit, or a push.
- Desired article behavior: investigate the reversal, explain pros and cons,
  place evidence beside the affected claim, and end with a useful verification
  action rather than a generic research note.

## Falsification and decision rules

The working claim fails if the original post cannot be attributed to an
OpenAI decision-maker, if current OpenAI documentation does not expose a
five-hour window for Plus, or if independent chronology contradicts the
reported Aug. 25 return. The phrase `five-hour limit` must not be expanded into
five hours of active compute, a fixed message count, or a guaranteed task
duration unless an official source defines it that way.

## Required source classes

1. Original announcement and prior first-person policy experiment.
2. First-party OpenAI role record and current pricing or help documentation.
3. Independent reporting for the Pacific-time chronology.
4. Attributable user observations for practical costs and counterevidence.
