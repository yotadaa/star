# Assignment: Bid-for-ad-slot websites

- Working topic: The recent cluster of small websites that sell a visible ad, link, or in-game placement through bidding, with `outbid.lol` and `flappybid.lol` as named examples.
- Reader question: Do these sites create useful advertising inventory, or do they mainly turn attention and auction mechanics into a short-lived internet game?
- Article form: Investigation with product-mechanics comparison and a bounded market analysis.
- Intended reader: Indie builders, small advertisers, growth practitioners, and technically curious readers who understand websites but do not need auction-design expertise.
- Language: `en-US`.

- Depth: Long-form, determined by the evidence rather than a target word count.

## Research questions

1. What does each named site sell, how does a bidder win, how long does the placement last, and what payment or refund rules are disclosed?
2. Which other live implementations belong to the same cluster, and which merely resemble it superficially?
3. What public social record supports describing this as a current phenomenon? What does the accessible record fail to establish about scale or trend status?
4. What can an advertiser measure, and what value can a site owner capture?
5. Where do moderation, spam, fraud, payment, permanence, legal, and brand-safety risks sit?
6. What evidence would disprove the working angle that these projects work better as participatory spectacle than as conventional ad inventory?

## Evidence threshold

The Research Gate requires direct inspection of the two named sites, at least two additional verified implementations or an explicitly narrowed comparison, multiple independent sources, a traceable social record, and primary legal/payment documentation for claims about auction, advertising, or platform risk. “Trending” will not appear unless a measurable source supports that term.
